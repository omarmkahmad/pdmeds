Pharmacokinetic parameters and sources

CF = levodopa-equivalent dose conversion factor (LED per mg). Cmax is per mg of drug, relative to the peak of 1 mg IR levodopa/carbidopa. Values marked ≈ are estimates fitted from prescribing information so the area under the curve matches the LED conversion factor.

Handled via the COMT setting, not as separate curves: entacapone (Comtan), opicapone (Ongentys), tolcapone (Tasmar). Not modeled (no accepted LED / non-dopaminergic mechanism): trihexyphenidyl, benztropine and other anticholinergics; pimavanserin (Nuplazid); zonisamide; sublingual apomorphine film (Kynmobi, withdrawn from the US market).

Model, assumptions and limitations

Model. Each dose is described by Cmax, Tmax and T½. Plasma levels rise linearly from the time of the dose to Cmax at Tmax, then halve every T½. Extended-release capsules (Rytary, Crexont) are modeled as an immediate-release fraction plus an extended-release fraction. Infusions (Duopa, Vyalev, Onapgo) rise exponentially toward a steady-state level set by the infusion rate and decay after the pump stops. Non-levodopa drugs are assigned pseudo-levodopa levels: Cmax is chosen so their AUC equals that of their levodopa-equivalent dose. Doses are assumed independent and additive.

Assumptions carried from the original tool. Agonists don’t literally become levodopa, and MAO-B inhibitors work by prolonging dopamine’s life rather than adding to it — treating them as constant additive LED is a deliberate sleight of hand for interpretability. Published PK parameters vary widely between studies, come from small samples, and mix healthy volunteers with PD patients, single doses with steady state, fed with fasted. Absorption lag, protein interference, gastroparesis, endogenous dopamine, and person-to-person variation are ignored. Pharmacokinetics (drug levels) is a proxy for what actually matters, pharmacodynamics (clinical effect).

Additions in this version. Rytary, Crexont, Inbrija, pramipexole ER, amantadine ER (Gocovri), bromocriptine, selegiline/Zelapar, safinamide, istradefylline, and the three continuous-delivery systems (Duopa, Vyalev, Onapgo) were parameterized from their prescribing information and the LED literature (Tomlinson 2010; Jost/MDS 2023 update) using the same AUC-matching logic. These are rougher estimates than the original tool’s referenced values — the istradefylline LED in particular is not established.
